After I read [Eva's review of the play](https://thatsaweb.site/films/the-trial/) I decided to give the book a go.
I have to say, as a disclaimer, that I'm not a huge fan of Kafka. I like the stories he tells but I'm not fond of his writing style.

This book was no different.

## This was a short story but a lengthy read

The story itself, being unfinished, isn't particularly long. A quick read in theory.
The story itself is also great - someone gets arrested, but is mostly left alone after a short interaction with the police.
What are they charged with? Unclear. How and when and where can they make their case? Obscure and opaque.

The reason why it took a good amount of energy and turned out mostly tedious instead of a joyful read is the way Kafka wrote it.
It meanders, it is verbose in places I wouldn't consider relevant and it adds detail that doesn't add much to the story.

That's of course to be expected - it's Kafka's style after all - but I still haven't warmed up to that. Having to take in and picture all the minute and absurd details, the hints and nods...
it takes capacity and attention away from taking in the sheer absurdity.

## The hilarity

It really is quite bizarre on a superficial layer. People being held in legal proceedings that are neither clear nor reasonable, eating up all their life.
At the same time, lawyers and such blowing things out of proportion and portraying it as the most important issue to be dealt with. The courts being stowed away in attics,
judges and court clerks being sex-hungry weirdos... it's a wild ride and a pretty ridiculous one.

## The dystopy

But there is the other layer. The subtle horror of being accused and processed by a system that is faceless, uncaring and absolutely intransparent.
Seeing how it consumes people left and right while at the same time being completely decoupled from the daily reality (the protagonist goes back to work, he carries out his life after all).
It suddenly showing up with hearings and - finally - a death penalty out of the blue, unblinking, unadjustable and unaccountable.

## My own experiences with bureaucracy

This does remind me of some gnarly occurrences of the bureacratic system in Germany rearing its ugly head. Of course not as bad as this, but not far from it, either.
Be it the uncaring "not our responsibility, go somewhere else" or the weird "This is broken, so you can't use it. But it's protected so you can't fix it either" logic of many situations in life there,
whenever the apparatus of bureaucracy catches wind of it. So this one hit home for me. Switzerland is _very_ different in that regard and this is one of the many reasons I left Germany behind, never to return again.

But I know that the risk to end up in absurd situations is never too far, if a system gets too detached from reality and becomes a purpose in and of itself.
