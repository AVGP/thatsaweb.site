## Perspective matters

Pictures say more than a thousand words, so, here:

![A field of sunflowers, beautiful but all more or less alike](/images/one-in-a-million.jpg)

It's a pretty picture. A field of sunflowers - their bright yellow heads looking at you, nice! But also a bit boring.

You've probably seen it many times before and if you lived here, it'd blend into the background. Nothing interesting to see here, right?

![A bee and a bumblebee sharing a sunflower together](/images/beauty-in-the-details.jpg)

This is from that same field, just a different perspective. Closer, more focused on a small piece. A single sunflower. 

It reveals beautiful structure and texture on the flower and, oh, there is even more life in this picture than meets the eye in the first photo: A bumblebee and a bee sharing this moment, collecting pollen and nectar side by side. Not competing, not collaborating either, just being in the moment (yuck, that last bit was a bit too much. Too cheesy.).

The point I'm trying to make is: The ordinary, the thing you saw dozens or hundreds of times still carry beauty and are still special, despite how normal and ordinary they may feel for you.

## Not just looking, seeing

I realised this for the first time when I was driving through Switzerland with a friend from abroad. I drove this road many times before and in the beginning I stopped sometimes to admire the view: The sprawling lake in front of the gorgeous mountains, capped with snow and ice. Stunning, really!

![A panoramic view of Lake Lucerne with the Alps in the background before sunset](/images/golden-hour-lake-and-mountains.jpg)

Yet, I stopped seeing it. I looked at this view, every couple of weeks I would drive by. But I wouldn't really notice, I didn't _see_ it anymore.

Until that day - my friend asked me to stop somewhere, so she could take a photo. I saw her expression, excitement. I looked at the photo and, for the first time in years, _I saw the vista_.

We can all benefit a lot from _learning to see_ again. Look at the people around you, look at the windows, look at the sky, at the plants and flowers around you. Take a proper look, as often as you can.

## The Chase

In the past couple of years I noticed something I've done for a very long time. I don't exactly know when it happened, but I think it might be around the time I turned 16. Before that, I enjoyed _time_. I walked across the fields, into the forest. Looked at the insects and fish in the streams and ponds. I strolled a lot, I looked at the sky every day. Then I started listening to the adults (bad idea) and did what they said was important (worse idea) and came up with a strategy, with a plan and did the work for that. I focused on the plan (terrible idea) and thought that's the most important thing (worst idea).

What happend in the years to come was, eventually (and after a series of horrible missteps and failures) success. Whee, success! But wait... something else happened as well. Whenever I reached a goal, completed a step in the plan, I didn't pause. I didn't relish or celebrate that moment. I immediately thought of the next step, the next goal. Whatever I accomplished vanished immediately. It was now normal, something that naturally happened - next!

## Enjoying the moment

While I was happy with where I was, how things went and my life in general, it felt off. Something wasn't right.
It was all superficial, it lacked depth and, now that I look back, it lacked _joy_. Of course I enjoyed vacations, job success, personal milestones.
But only now, years later, I feel _gratitude_ and _deep, meaningful happiness_.

What I didn't notice in all this chasing - always aiming for the next step, working on the next goal - is that I needed to pause and let things catch up.

I want to emphasize this: **Let life catch up**.

Once I did this, paused, looked back and inside, I realised all the beauty and the joy that I had run past.

![Zurich's oldtown from the air - could be a postcard](/images/downtown-zurich-aerial.jpg)

I mean: I married the love of my life, we celebrated with all the people who mattered so much for us. We live in a beautiful place. I published a book! I went to the place I only could dream of visiting. I learned to dive. So many things happened, each full of meaning, impact and joy.

Yet I didn't see it. It was behind me while I spend energy chasing the next thing. **Don't do that**.
Of course, having a plan and working towards it is the right thing to do - I continue to do that, too. But don't _rush_.

Celebrate your wins. Heal from your failures. Pause and admire the view.
