This week my body prepared for a war that never came.

Nothing was chasing me. That is the part I kept having to explain to my own hands. There were deadlines, yes. Three websites moving houses at the same time, a company being born on paper, a heatwave pressing on the windows, decisions arriving faster than I could unwrap them. Busy. Genuinely, properly busy. But busy is not danger, and somewhere in me sits a very old employee who cannot tell the difference.

I call her the guard. She was hired years ago, trained by circumstances I did not choose, and she took the training seriously. Her whole job description is one word: braced. She has never once missed a shift.

So when the week got loud, she did what she was trained to do:

```
> SENTINEL.EXE v1.988 loading...
> threat scan .......... ACTIVE
> source of threat ..... NOT FOUND
> escalating anyway (per original configuration)
> deploying: shoulder tension, jaw, 3am rehearsals
  of conversations that will never happen,
  the feeling of being left behind
> note: this software has not been updated
  since installation
```

That last line is the whole story. The software was never wrong. It was just written for a world I no longer live in.

A nervous system is not a liar. It is a historian with the volume stuck on high.

Here is what surviving looked like, in case you imagine something cinematic: it looked like going outside twice in one day for no reason except that outside exists. It looked like saying out loud, to no one, "we are not in danger, we are in demand," and hearing how different those two words actually are. It looked like doing the work anyway, well, while the alarm rang, and refusing to accept the alarm's interpretation of events. The alarm rang all week. The war never showed up. Both things are true, and learning to hold both at once is, I think, what healing actually is on a weekday.

There are things I have been trying to put down for three years now. I will not list them here; some doors I open only for the people I choose, in rooms I choose, and that is not secrecy, that is ownership. What I will tell you is why it never worked before: I kept trying to put them down with the same hands that were trained to grip. You cannot release something while braced. For three years I had the intention and not the equipment.

The equipment arrived slowly, and from three directions at once. Realisations that landed hard enough to rearrange furniture. Kindness towards myself, which I can now admit was the most difficult skill I have ever practised, harder than any technical thing I have ever learned. And the eyes of the people in my life who truly see me: not the polished version, the whole one. It turns out you cannot find your true self entirely alone. Some of it only becomes visible in the reflection of people who refuse to look away.

I realised years ago that vulnerability is raw power in this world. I am telling you this because I intend to be among the strongest.

And now the part I did not expect to write.

Last month I told you about the room. How I healed here, and how I had healed enough to feel its walls. The room has not changed since then. But some mornings it feels smaller than my wings, and for a while I thought that feeling was the whole message. It is not. Because while the guard was standing at the door scanning for enemies, I finally noticed what the child in me had been doing with all that wall space.

She has been drawing.

```
        ☼
   ~        ~
  ┌───────────────┐
  │ ┌──┐   ┌──┐  │     a city by the sea
  │ │  │   │  │  │     a desk facing a window
  │ └──┘   └──┘  │     a door that locks from MY side
  └───────────────┘
 ≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈≈
```

Crayon on plaster. A city salt in the air. An apartment with a desk where the light lands. A year that already stands on solid ground, so that everything built inside it belongs to the future instead of to survival.

For years this daydreaming would feel chaotic and not something to look forward to, because I was trying to control the narrative. Once taste raw freedom, you begin wonderding how free you feel within it. This week, something else happened. The adult in me walked over to the wall with a pencil and a calculator, looked at the drawings for a long time, and started connecting them. This drawing is a number, and I know it exactly. This one is a month on a real calendar. This one is work that carries into next year, agreed with people who negotiated and met me, and found me un-shrinkable. Line by line, the crayon became a blueprint. Not a fantasy with a deadline. A plan with a budget.

The child draws the destination. The adult builds the road. The guard, it turns out, was never the enemy: she just needs new work.

So I am not firing her. You do not fire someone who kept you alive. I am retraining her. Less door-watching, more perimeter walks around the actual construction site. And the new arrangement, in the only format this website respects:

```
> EVA v2.0 :: changelog
+ weekends returned to their rightful owner
+ rest reclassified: maintenance, not weakness
+ kindness to self installed as default, not exception
+ war-readiness budget reallocated to drawing supplies
- removed: the guard's chair by the door
  (the guard stays; she is learning to sit by the window instead)
```

Last month I ended by telling you I can walk now. That is still true. But walking, it turns out, was never the final feature. It was just the first thing you do in a room before you understand what your legs are actually for.

The child draws. The adult connects. The guard is learning to sit.

We are not at war. We are at work.
