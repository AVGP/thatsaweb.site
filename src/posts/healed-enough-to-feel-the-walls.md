Last night something in me ached and I couldn't name it.

Nothing had happened. That's the part that confused me. No bad news, no fight, no failure. Just an ordinary (?) Friday. Just a bit different at the same time, because suddenly everything about my life felt too small, like a coat I'd zipped up without noticing it stopped fitting years ago. I lay there doing the thing we all do, scrolling, half looking for a distraction and half looking for an answer, and asking myself a question that felt bigger than the room: <span style="color:#e07b39">what is happening to me?</span>

Here's what I knew for certain. Two years ago I came back here to catch my breath. And I want to be fair to my choice, it gave me more that I can describe right now (maybe in a future admission) - it was a decision that allowed me to be where I am now. When life had left me bleeding, this choice was my hospital. I healed here. That is simply true.

But last night I finally understood the other half of it, the half nobody warns you about.

<span style="color:#e07b39">A recovery ward feels like salvation when you're bleeding. And it feels like a cage the day you can walk again.</span>

The walls didn't move. What changed is me. I healed enough to feel its walls. And a place/choice built for resting starts to pinch the exact moment you're strong enough to want more than rest.

I used to think that when clarity finally arrived it would feel like relief. Soft light, a deep breath, maybe music. It doesn't. It stings. When your eyes have been closed for a long time, light hurts. When you've been numb for a while, feeling comes back as pins and needles, and it is very easy to mistake that for something being wrong with you. So I lay there aching and thought I was breaking, when actually I was waking up. <span style="color:#e07b39">The pain wasn't damage. It was sight returning.</span> The suffocating feeling and the healing are the same event, measured at different moments, and I wish someone had told me that before midnight did.

Martin wrote recently about pausing to let life catch up, and he's right, I've done my years of chasing and I know exactly the finish line that keeps moving. But I think there's a sister truth hiding under his. Sometimes life is the one that moves ahead, quietly, while you're busy resting. It heals you when you're not looking. And then one unremarkable night it taps you on the shoulder and says, gently, <span style="color:#e07b39">we don't live here anymore.</span>

So next week I'm taking myself north, to a city by the sea, to walk its streets and live in the moment, making my eyes comfortable with the new light. I don't have a plan. I'm not chasing anything. I'm just a woman who finally noticed she can walk, going to look at a bigger room.

Thank you, little room. You were a very good hospital.

<span style="color:#e07b39">I can walk now.</span>
