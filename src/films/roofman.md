This is a movie that is hard to grasp in a single sentence.

![The poster - is this gonna be fun? Action? Serious?](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/c/c0/Roofman_poster.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)

It started with the poster. This man with a gun, carrying a huge plushie on his shoulders. Is it going to be a heist movie? Fun action comedy?
I kind of expected a fun quick watch, not more, not less.

## Smart but unadjusted

The protagonist - ex-military - struggles to fit into society. We start off with seeing him unable to buy a bike for his daughter on her birthday.
We're seeing that he is extremely observant and quite clever, but somehow can't turn his abilities into wealth. It's not explained why that is - I would've liked to see this part fleshed out a bit more. 

Was he just a misfit, were there no opportunities, did something else lead to this? It's a bit unfortunate, but we see him struggle - he tries to reconcile with his ex-wife, he tries to be a good dad to his kids. If he's working so hard on being a good guy, why isn't the part where you work towards your goals
in the job market part of that?

## Roofman era

Anyway, we're seeing him realising how he can make money quickly by breaking into McDonald's restaurant through the roof, grab money from the safe before it gets collected and get a head-start by locking employees into the freezer. 
He does carry a gun doing these robberies but he's doing his best to remain polite and helpful. Apologising to the staff, making sure they wear warm clothes when they go into the freezer, calling the police to make sure they get freed soon enough - so yeah, this is kind of a "friendly robber".

While this is clearly showing that our protagonist tries to minimize damage, I'm not really buying that. It's still a dangerous and traumatic situation to put the employees into.

And, yes, he gets caught eventually.

## Prison break and hiding

He gets sentenced with a hefty, very long time behind bars. Which he feels is unfair. He got "slammed". 
It's clear he doesn't really see the harm he caused - and I'm willing to ignore the financial damage for the moment here.
Being surprised by a masked guy holding a gun is no joke, no matter how friendly they might be.

His ex-wife and kids are now even harder to reach - and he clearly misses them dearly.

But our guy is clever. He realises that prison is just another building with people and routines. So eventually he finds a way to escape.
I gotta say: This is a great bit of plot. There's a clear mental and phyiscal investment our "genius" protagonist makes.
The reward is an escape without any use of force, free of threats or violence. This is genuinely great.

He eventually finds a hiding spot and manages, again through investing a bunch of effort, to set up a somewhat permanent camp in a store, more or less "hidden in plain sight". He even learns about the employees there and takes interest in the harsh character of the manager and the very gentle character of one of the people working in the store.

## More lies

After the manager turns down the gentle, friendly employee's request for a donation to a toy drive of their church community, our protagonist comes out of hiding. And donates stolen toys. He becomes part of the church community and, uh _befriends_ the wonderful employee he kept watching at their work through cameras at his hiding spot.

We learn that Leigh, said gentle and friendly person, has been hurt before and struggles to keep things together with her divorce and her two daughters.
She's careful in who she chooses to let into her and her kids' life - and still, our "friendly" protagonist chooses to pursue a deeper connection to the three of them. All based on a lie and the knowledge (at least I assume he knows this, he's _smart_ after all) that this will not work out in the long run.

He's a fugitive, he's introduced himself as someone completely different and lies to them about pretty much all the things. He now also steals massively from the store to keep up his lies. At no point do we see him trying to make a living doing honest work. Not once.

## The finale

He finally tries to get away from it all, robs the toy store he's been hiding in to get the money for fake documents....and then decides to say his final goodbye to the "family" he made himself part of. But Leigh caught on and the police captures him - so he's back in prison. With an even longer sentence now.

We see him in some sort of therapy session in prison and he finally admits that he's where he belongs - that he won't try to escape again. And that he shouldn't have focused so much on material gifts for the people close to him - just being there for them might have been the right thing all along.

## Huh. Now what?

Well, finally we find out: He tried to escape prison twice after this. What the flying fuck.

I don't know how to feel about this film, to be honest. One one hand I _want to like the guy_. The film does everything in its power to try to portray this as some sort of ["Oceans Eleven"](https://popfeed.social/movie/161) kinda fun, harmless, smart heist story. I really wish I could see it as such.

But instead I'm seeing someone who put innocent people in actual danger (there were weapons involved after all) for personal gain. He is portrayed as "caring for his family" - but then, once he realises he can't get in touch with his kids without potential recapture, he seems to give up on them entirely. Sure, we're seeing him holding that family photo near and dear, but _that's about it_. 

He then gets close to a mother and her kids - and wins their trust... but then tries to escape alone again? Out of the blue. No warning, no wrap up. What the _fuck_? Overall he seems to truly care only about himself - sure, he makes all the gestures and _seems_ to be caring and kind... but if it comes down to it, he cares about himself. In way he's portrayed as a child in the body of a grown man, too - he feeds on candy in the toy store and sleeps amongst plushies.

And then the whole _genius_ angle - that's the bit I can believe the most, actually. I met someone once, back in school. Brilliant dude, truly smart - but also self-sabotaging and putting effort in questionable activities, at best. I happen to know that he turned things around and made truly good decisions later on, so that's great. I wanted this story to be something like that - but it just isn't. All I'm seeing is someone focusing on material values and expending great effort in the entirely wrong way.

This is based on [an actual person and their story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Manchester) - I dunno how I missed this at the beginning but holy fuck. While the movie certainly dramatized bits and pieces, the general vibe seems to be taken from that biography.
