Well, if you're looking for something fun, may I recommend [Bullet Train](/films/bullet-train) instead?
This film is heavy and dark and deep. It basically starts off the way it ends: With death and the end of life.

## Life imitates art imitates life...

We're following the life of a theater director. It begins with his health and his relationship deteriorating - with strange things happening around him.
Soon it's clear that his perception and reality have a tentative relationship. As we start with a clock showing us a time, we note that time doesn't seem to pass in normal ways here.
Suddenly his (ex-)wife and daugther aren't gone a week, but months now. Maybe years. She's no longer four, she's almost eleven - and then she's an adult.

So he starts putting together a play. A play that's supposed to be brutally honest about him and his life. He keeps growing the set, the plot, the cast... and things start to blur.
What is play? What is happening now? What has happened in the past, real or in the play? Who is the man who kept watching the protagonist? Why is the protagonist in the TV series and movies?

## Things fall apart

![They are moving apart](https://film-grab.com/wp-content/uploads/photo-gallery/08%20(969).jpg)

Not only does his health deteriorate and his body starting to sabotage him - he also sabotages and loses the relationships he's so keen on.
One after another turns away. Some return, some don't. Some things repeat - just with different protagonists around our actual protagonist.

## Inside and outside and everything in between

Eventually he's in the set, he's part of his own play. And then he realises: The extras aren't extras, they each have their own story, their own lives. He's not special, he's not different.
And then he notices a set inside the set... in which he finds a set.

## The end is built into the beginning

Along the way, he let someone else portray him in the play. He also joins the play, but as someone else. He's also taking direction through an ear piece.
Eventually he gets back with Hazel, who he had a brief relationship with as she worked in the box office of his theater. Back in the beginning, she moved into a perpetually burning house. 
She liked it, but was worried about dying from the fire - the decision of one's own death is an important one after all - but buys the house anyway. 

As she says "The end is built into the beginning" she dies of smoke inhalation. Death was the beginning of this movie, death was a concern at the beginning of her life in this house.
And as our protagonist moves around in the ruins of his life - and his play - he figures out when to actually screen his big magnus opum... and gets his final cue through the ear piece: Die.

## Time...

![A clock at 7:45](https://film-grab.com/wp-content/uploads/photo-gallery/01%20(970).jpg)

What's weird is that we're seeing clocks a few times. And it might just be coincidence but I'm wondering if the end he experiences at the beginning - his relationship falling apart, someone on the radio talking about autumn and death...
did we just see the minute before his death? Or is this showing that our whole life passes us by, if we're trying to control, to chase the unobtainable, to stick with the path (as he keeps chasing his ex-wife and the relationships he lost).

He tries to shape life by building a staged version of it. To the point where the staged version has a staged version, completely losing focus of the actual life.

I'm not sure what the deeper meaning is, but I admire that depending on your point of view, you will find a layer of meaning that works for you. But either way: It's damn dark and depressing.
